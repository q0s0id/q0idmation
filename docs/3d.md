# 3D in q0editor

q0editor has a 3D engine built in. You can model, rig and animate 3D models, or import them from FBX, glTF/GLB and OBJ files. Then use them in any animation: as 3D pieces inside a 2D scene, or in a fully 3D scene with a moving camera. 3D lights light models and also 2D drawings, and suns and spotlights cast shadows onto both.

The same renderer runs in the editor, in q0player (Windows, Android, desktop) and in exports, so a scene looks the same everywhere.

## Getting a model

- **File > Import 3D Model...** adds an FBX, GLB, glTF or OBJ file to the Library and places it in the middle of the stage. Dropping the file onto the window does the same.
  - Meshes, materials, textures (embedded or next to the file), bones, skin weights and every animation take come in.
  - Imported models are sized to half the stage's height. Change that under **On the stage > Size** in the model editor.
- **File > New 3D Model** starts from a cube and opens the model editor.
- Double-click a model in the Library, or press **Edit model...** in its Properties, to open it in the model editor.

## The model editor

The model editor takes the whole window. Nothing you do there touches the project until you press **Save to library**. Saving is one undo step of the project, and it updates every placement of that model. **Close** asks before throwing away unsaved changes. **?** in the top bar lists every mouse move and key.

The screen:
- **Left**: what you work on (Parts, Shape or Bones), the tools, and the actions for that mode.
- **Top**: the active tool's settings (axes, snapping, which shape to add...).
- **Right**: the outliner (parts and bones, with eye toggles to hide parts), exact numbers for what is picked, materials, and how big the model is on the stage.
- **Bottom**: animation clips and the timeline.
- **Viewport**: a floor grid that follows the camera and fades into the distance, so there is always ground to work on. A hint along the bottom says what the tool does and what the keys do right now. Right-click (without dragging) opens the actions at hand.

**Looking around**
- Drag with the right or middle mouse button (or Alt + left) to orbit; add Shift to slide the view; the wheel or a pinch zooms.
- The ball in the top-right corner: drag it to orbit, click X, Y or Z to look along that axis. Below it are buttons to slide, zoom (drag them), frame everything and switch perspective off.
- F frames what is picked, Home frames everything.

**Work on** (Tab switches Parts and Shape, Ctrl+Tab goes to Bones)
- **Parts**: whole meshes.
- **Shape**: one part's points (1), edges (2) or faces (3).
- **Bones**: the skeleton and its animation.

**Tools** (each on a key)

| Tool | Key | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| Select | Q | Click to pick, Shift-click to add, drag a box. Only what you can see is picked, unless **See through** is on. |
| Move | W | A gizmo: drag an arrow to move along it, a square to slide in its plane, the dot to move freely. You can also drag anything straight away. |
| Rotate | E | Drag a coloured ring to turn about its axis, the outer ring to turn toward you. |
| Scale | R | Drag a handle to stretch along it, the middle to size evenly. |
| Add shape | Shift A | Pick a cube, sphere, cylinder, cone, torus, capsule or plane in the top bar, then click on the floor or on a surface to put it there. Sizes fit the view unless you set one. In Shape mode the shape joins the part you are shaping. |
| Extrude | T | Pick faces, then drag the orange arrow to pull them out. |
| Inset | I | Drag to shrink the picked faces into a new ring. |
| Loop cut | C | Point at an edge to see where the new loop goes (yellow), click to cut, or drag to slide it first. |
| Paint | P | Pick a material in the top bar, then click or drag over faces (Shape mode) or parts (Parts mode). |
| Draw bones | B | Drag to draw a bone. The next one grows from the picked bone's tip; click empty space to start a new chain. |

**Axes** in the top bar: **Model** moves along the model's axes, **Own** along the picked part's or bone's own.

While a change is running:
- The amount shows next to the pointer.
- Ctrl snaps (or tick **Snap**), in grid squares or angle steps.
- G moves the pick with the mouse without a gizmo; X, Y or Z then keeps it to an axis, Shift with one keeps it out of that axis.
- Let go (or click / Enter after G) to finish; Esc or right-click cancels.
- Ctrl+Z / Ctrl+Y undo and redo inside the editor.

**Actions**
- Parts: Duplicate (Shift D), Delete (X), Join, Shade smooth/flat.
- Shape: Fill (F), Merge (M), Delete (X), Duplicate (Shift D), Weld close points, Subdivide, Smooth (rounded subdivision), Triangulate, Mirror x/y/z, Flip faces, Faces outward, Texture positions (Box, Front, Around), Pick all (A), none (Alt A), linked (L).
- Bones: Add bone, Delete bone, **Bind parts to bones** (automatic weights from the nearest bones), Unbind.

**Bones and animation**
- With **Edit skeleton** on (top bar), moving and turning bones shapes the skeleton itself.
- Otherwise turning or moving a bone keys the current clip at the playhead. K keys the picked bone as it is; **Key all bones** keys every bone.

**Materials**
- Swatches pick the material; **+** adds one.
- Colour, a picture (texture), roughness, metal and glow.
- Looks: **Smooth** lighting, **Cartoon** (flat bands of light, like cel animation) or **Flat** colour (ignores light).
- **Use on picked** gives the material to the picked faces or parts; the Paint tool does it by hand.

**On the stage**
- **Size** sets stage pixels per model unit.
- **Cartoon outline** draws an ink line of a set width around the whole model, at the same width however near or far it is.

**Animation bar**
- Make clips ("walk", "jump"...), rename them and set their length.

**Smooth shading** keeps sharp edges: faces that meet at more than 60° stay creased, so a smooth box keeps its edges while a ball or a cylinder's side shades round.
- Buttons go to the first, previous, next and last frame; Space or the play button plays, with or without looping.
- Click or drag the timeline to scrub. Diamonds show the picked bone's keys; dots show all keys.

## Models in an animation

A placed model is a normal placement:
- Move, size and rotate it with the usual tools.
- Tween it, mask it, put it inside symbols.

Its Properties panel has a **3D** section:
- **Depth**: how far back it sits (more is further away).
- **Lean back** and **Turn**: rotate it in 3D. The usual rotation spins it on the screen.
- **Lit** by the 3D lights.
- **Clip**, **Start at**, **Speed**, **Loop**: which animation plays from that keyframe.
  - Speed 0 holds the pose at "Start at". Keying "Start at" on two keyframes with a tween scrubs through the clip.
- **Pose**: pick a bone and turn it.
  - Each change keys that bone on the current frame, eased between keys.
  - On top of the clip, so you can bend an arm while a walk plays.
  - **Back to the clip** removes the pose keys.

All 3D values are kept per keyframe and tween like position and opacity do.

## 3D tools on the stage

Once a project has anything in 3D, a **3D** bar shows in the stage's top-left corner. It holds four tools, each with the same gizmo as in the model editor:
- **Move in 3D**:
  - Drag an arrow to move along it, a square to slide in its plane, the dot to move freely.
  - Dragging the object itself also moves it freely.
  - When the depth arrow points straight at you, a **depth** knob under the gizmo pushes the object back or pulls it near.
- **Turn in 3D**: drag a ring to turn about its axis, the outer ring to turn toward you.
- **Size in 3D**: drag a handle to stretch along it, the middle to size evenly.
- **3D view**: drag to look around the stage. See below.

Click to pick what to work on: the nearest thing under the pointer.

These tools work on drawings too: pushing one back or turning it places it in 3D on its own. Hold Ctrl to snap: to 10 px, 15° and steps of 10%. **own axes** in the bar moves along the object's own axes instead of the stage's. Picking any 2D tool goes back to the 2D tools and the scene camera.

### The 3D view

The **3D view** tool looks at the stage from a camera of the editor's own:
- **Moving the view:**
  - Drag to orbit.
  - Shift-drag to slide.
  - The wheel comes closer.
  - With the other 3D tools, right or middle drag orbits too.
- **What you see:**
  - Everything on the stage is drawn in 3D. Flat drawings are cards on the paper, in their layer order.
  - A floor grid sits at the stage's bottom edge.
  - The scene camera shows in blue with the part of the stage it frames.
- **Quick views:** the bar's **front**, **top**, **side** and **3/4** jump to set views, and **camera** looks through the scene camera again.

The 3D view only changes how you see the stage, never the animation.

## Drawings in 3D

Tick **Place in 3D** in any placement's 3D section. The drawing, bitmap, video or symbol then becomes a card standing in 3D:
- Push it back with Depth, turn it with Lean back / Turn.
- Light it with the 3D lights.
- Models and other cards hide behind it by depth.

## The 3D window (camera and lights)

Open it with **3D** in the menu bar.

**Camera**
- Off, the stage camera looks straight at the stage, so anything at depth 0 looks exactly as drawn.
- **Own camera** gives the timeline a camera you can move, aim, roll and zoom, with keys on frames for camera moves.
  - The arrow buttons orbit, raise, lower and dolly it.
  - **No perspective** makes far things keep their size.

**Light**
- **Everywhere** is the ambient light.
- **Bulb** (shines all round, fading over its reach), **Spot** (a cone) and **Sun** (parallel light).
  - Each has a colour, brightness, reach, cone and edge.
  - Keys animate them.
  - **Ride with selected** makes a light move and turn with an object.
  - **Looks like** sets up Studio, Sunset or Night lighting in one click.
- **Light flat drawings too**: the 3D lights also light ordinary 2D artwork, on every layer or up to a chosen one.
  - The art lies where it is drawn, at depth 0, facing the camera, and stays in layer order.
  - Models still stand in front of it.
- **Shadows**: suns and spots with **casts shadows** throw shadows from models and 3D cards onto everything lit, flat drawings included.

While the window is open, the lights show on the stage. Drag a light, or the ring it aims at, to move it at its depth.

Without any 3D lights, models get a soft default light and drawings stay exactly as drawn.

## Glass

Pick **Glass** as a material's **Look** in the model editor. It works like this:
- It lets what is behind it show through, tinted by its colour.
- It mirrors a soft studio around it, more the more it is seen edge-on, and catches sharp highlights from the lights.
- **Clear** sets how much shows through: 1 is clear glass, 0 is milky.

Glass draws the same in the editor, q0player and exports.

## Physics in 3D

**To give an object a body**, find **Physics** in its 3D properties and tick **has a body**. The object needs to be a model or placed in 3D. Then set:
- whether it **falls** or **stays put**
- its **shape**: its **box**, or a **ball**
- **Bounce**, **Grip** and **Weight**
- a **Push** and a **Spin** to start with

**The world**, in the 3D window's **Physics** section:
- **Gravity**, pulling down the stage.
- A **floor** at the stage's bottom edge, lower by an offset if you like.

**Bake falls** simulates from the current frame for the given number of frames. It keys every falling body on every frame after it, position and turn. A bake is one undo step, and it plays the same everywhere because it is ordinary keys.

## Liquids

**+ Pour a liquid** in the 3D window's **Physics** section adds a block of liquid over the middle of the stage, drawn with the current layer. Set:
- **Looks**:
  - its colour
  - **clear** like water (it is drawn as glass, its colour's opacity says how deep the tint is) or **thick** like paint
- **Block from** / **Block to**: the corners of the block it starts as.
- **Drops apart**: smaller is smoother and slower.
- **Splash**: a speed to start with.
- **Thickness**: water to honey.
- **Cling**: how much it holds together in drops.
- **Tank**: walls round the stage keep it in.

**Bake liquid** lets it go on the current frame:
- It falls, splashes over the floor and over every body as it stands on that frame.
- The drops of every frame are kept with the project.
- A smooth surface is drawn over them, the same way in every renderer.
- Before the bake's first frame there is no liquid; after its last, the last frame holds.

## Haze

**Haze** in the 3D window's **Light** section fades far 3D things into a colour:
- They are clear up to **Starts at** and fully hazed at **Thick at**, both in stage pixels from the camera.
- It gives depth to big scenes, and mist or night to small ones.

## How layers and depth mix

Placements are drawn in layer order, as always. Neighbouring 3D items (models, cards) are drawn together and hide each other by real depth. A flat drawing that comes after them in layer order is drawn over them, and one that comes before is drawn under them. Lit flat drawings keep their layer order too.

## Limits

- **Point (bulb) lights** do not cast shadows; suns and spots do, two of them per frame.
- **Faded layers** (script-faded) fade their 2D art, but not the 3D models on them.
- **Selection** of a model or card on the stage uses its unturned outline.
- **FBX features not imported**: NURBS, blend shapes (morph targets), constraints and cameras.
- **glTF textures**: only the base colour picture is used.
- **Inside a symbol placed in 3D**, models are shown flat-on (without perspective) in that card's picture.
